# Returning to Development After a Year Away: My Experience with Modern AI Tools

For the past year, I stepped away from programming to focus on business obligations in Southeast Asia. Getting back into development felt both familiar and unfamiliar—familiar in the fundamentals, but unfamiliar in how quickly the ecosystem has evolved, especially with AI becoming a core part of modern development workflows.

One of the first things I explored was the landscape of AI-powered IDEs and coding agents. Tools like **Cursor** have been gaining attention, so naturally I tried it out. While the interface was clean and the workflow promising, the free tier felt restrictive. I also came across reports from other developers on Reddit mentioning unexpected overages—some even paying double their Pro subscription costs.

After experimenting for a while, I decided to give **GitHub Copilot** a serious try. At first, I wasn’t fully convinced. The suggestions felt hit-or-miss, and I wasn’t sure it would actually improve my workflow. But after spending more time with it, watching tutorials, and understanding how to guide the model, it started to click. The productivity boost was real.

To put these tools to the test, I decided to rebuild my GitHub profile web page—this time using **React**. With Copilot assisting and a refreshed mindset, I was able to complete the entire project in under a day. The experience reminded me how much I enjoy building clean, responsive frontends and how these AI tools can streamline brainstorming, boilerplate creation, and even debugging.

Coming back after a year away has been exciting. The ecosystem has matured, AI integration feels more seamless, and I’m looking forward to diving deeper into modern frontend workflows, tooling, and best practices. This is just the beginning of my return to engineering—and I’m glad to be back.
